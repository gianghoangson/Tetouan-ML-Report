\begin{abstract}
Distributors need a forecast of every hour of the next day to plan electricity purchases. This project forecasts the hourly load of Tetouan City (sum of three distribution zones, 2017) one day ahead with five conventional models (linear regression, decision tree, random forest, SVR and XGBoost) and compares them with seasonal naive baselines. Evaluation is strictly chronological: five expanding-window folds on January--November are used for every decision, and December is predicted once as the test set. Cross-validation reveals that tree models fail during the July heat wave because they cannot predict above the training range; learning the deviation from yesterday's load fixes this. Daily lags are the most useful feature group, while weekly and rolling features hurt. The SVR selected on cross-validation reaches a December RMSE of 1,625 kW and MAPE of 1.78\%, 50.7\% better than the ``tomorrow equals today'' baseline. A leakage experiment shows that shuffled splits and ten-minute horizons, common in public notebooks, inflate R$^2$ to 0.999 with the same model. Results assume measured target-hour weather and cover a single year.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} electricity demand; Tetouan City; time-series validation; support vector regression; feature engineering; data leakage.
