# 02 – Feature engineering

Guideline sections 4–5. Fix the problem definition, build the feature matrices for both horizons, check for leakage, and produce **T02** (split and folds), **T03** (feature list) and **F10** (split diagram).

Features are built on the **whole chronological series** and the split is applied afterwards. This is safe because every history feature is shifted by at least the forecast horizon, so a row only uses its own past.

## 1. Problem definition (guideline section 4)

| Item | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Target | `Total = Z1 + Z2 + Z3`, mean load per hour (kW) | City-level quantity; unaffected by transfers between zones (DQ01, F02) |
| Resolution | 1 hour | Planning unit; less noise; keeps SVR tractable |
| Main horizon | 24 h (day-ahead): issued at 00:00 for the 24 hours of the day | What a distribution company needs to plan the next day |
| Secondary horizon | 1 h | Comparison with the literature; effect of the horizon |
| Split | Train Jan–Oct · validation Nov · test Dec (by date) | No temporal leakage |

**Decisions taken from the EDA.** (1) Public holidays are split into `is_religious_holiday` (Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Islamic New Year, Mawlid) and `is_civil_holiday`, because F08 shows very different effects; the classification uses the holiday *type*, known in advance, not December data. (2) No daylight-saving feature: `lag_24` and `lag_168` already carry the most recent profile.

**Weather assumption.** Weather is used at the forecast hour, i.e. a perfect weather forecast is assumed; this is a limitation (notebook 06 measures how much the models depend on weather).

**Model features.** `make_features()` builds every column, but the models use **feature set D** (`MODEL_FEATURES`: weather, calendar, special days, `lag_24`, `lag_48`; 17 features for h24 and 19 for h1). `lag_168` and the rolling means are still built for the ablation and the Naive-168h baseline. This choice was made later, on dev CV (notebooks 05–06): set D beat the full set E for 8 of 10 model/target combinations, because those features carry the absolute load level and force tree models to extrapolate in the July heat wave.

## 3. Leakage checks

`check_no_leakage()` recomputes **every** lag and rolling feature by hand at 200 random timestamps and compares them with `X`. The cell also checks that no NaN is left and that no forbidden column is present (`month`, `dayofyear`, or the zones / total at the same timestamp).

--- CELL 8 (code; execution=3) ---
FORBIDDEN = {'month', 'dayofyear', 'Z1', 'Z2', 'Z3', 'Total'}
for name, X, h in [('h24', X24, 24), ('h1', X1, 1)]:
    assert check_no_leakage(X, full_y, horizon=h, n_checks=200)
    assert X.isna().sum().sum() == 0
    assert not FORBIDDEN & set(X.columns), FORBIDDEN & set(X.columns)
    assert min(int(c.split('_')[1]) for c in X if c.startswith('lag_')) >= h
    print(f'{name}: all leakage checks passed')

# The ablation feature sets must exist in the day-ahead matrix

**Observations.** Training has 7,105 hours (296 days), validation 720 and test 720 (30 days, the data end on 30 December). The five folds use an **expanding window**: training always starts on 8 January and grows by 30 days per fold, validation covers the next 30 days, from 4 July to 30 November. Fold 0 (4 July–2 August) is the hottest, highest-load period with the least training data, so it will be the hardest. **Fold 4 is exactly the Level 1 split** (train to 31 October, validate November).
